Executive summary

Medical consumables and disposables — syringes and needles, gloves, IV administration and infusion sets, catheters and tubes, wound care, surgical drapes and disposables, respiratory consumables — are the highest-count, highest-frequency purchases in any hospital. Published estimates for the 2025 global market range from USD 114 bn to USD 504 bn; our central estimate is USD 120 bn, growing at about 6% to USD 175 bn by 2032.

After the pandemic’s price spikes (gloves, masks, syringes) and the subsequent collapse, prices have normalised but supply chains have changed: more regional manufacturing (India, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt), safety-engineered devices mandated by infection-control rules, and GS1 barcoding demanded by hospital groups. For buyers this is a category where consolidation, specification discipline and landed-cost benchmarking deliver quickly.

Key facts and context

  • Plastic resin is 57.0% of medical-disposables raw material; hospitals are 54.8% of end-use demand (2025). (IMARC Group)
  • Drug-delivery disposables (syringes, IV sets, infusion) are the largest product category at 17.24% of global market (2025). (IMARC Group)
  • Global glove production capacity projected >500 bn pieces/yr in FY2026-27 against ~400 bn demand. (New Straits Times / CIMB)
  • Malaysia holds 65-70% of the US glove market; China 1-2% after 110% combined tariffs. (New Straits Times)
  • Saudi Arabia had ~1.3 million elderly people in 2024 (~5% of population), cited as a disposables driver. (IMARC Group)
  • General & laparoscopic surgery accounts for ~39% of GCC medical consumables demand (2026). (MarkNtel Advisors)
  • India launched a National Single Window System for medical device approvals (Jan 2024), built by TCS. (IMARC Group)
  • Global disposable medical devices (device scope) grow 4.9% CAGR 2025-2030 to USD 144.85 bn. (MarketsandMarkets)

GCC and India outlook

GCC

Imports dominate but local manufacturing is growing (gloves, syringes, IV fluids and sets in Saudi Arabia and the UAE); NUPCO and PureHealth/Rafed frameworks set reference prices for the public sector; private groups benchmark against them.

India

India is both a large market and a major exporter (Hindustan Syringes, Romsons, Polymed, Poly Medicure, Becton Dickinson India, Mitra Industries); Indian consumables with CE/ISO 13485 are routinely 30–50% below European equivalents landed in the GCC.

Market dynamics

Growth drivers

  • Infection-control mandates and HAI reduction driving single-use adoption (disposables ~48% of GCC consumables by material)
  • Rising surgical volumes, esp. minimally invasive/laparoscopic (general & laparoscopic surgery ~39% of GCC consumables)
  • Chronic disease burden (diabetes, CVD, orthopaedics) raising drug-delivery disposable demand (fastest-growing device type)
  • GCC hospital-network expansion under Saudi Vision 2030 and NUPCO centralised procurement framework agreements
  • India PLI scheme and Make in India driving domestic syringe/glove manufacturing (India market 8.49% CAGR)

Restraints

  • Heavy import reliance in GCC creating supply-chain vulnerability and price swings
  • High cost of advanced/specialised consumables (antimicrobial dressings, safety-engineered devices)
  • Global glove oversupply: capacity >500 bn pieces vs ~400 bn demand through FY2027, compressing manufacturer margins
  • Tariff volatility: US 110% combined tariff on Chinese gloves (100% Section 301 + 10% Section 122 from Feb 2026) redirecting trade flows
  • Environmental pressure on plastic (57% of raw material) and medical-waste regulation

Opportunities

  • Local GCC manufacturing via NUPCO long-term framework agreements for IV sets, gloves, syringes, wound care (2026)
  • PureHealth-Sinopharm expanded partnership for local distribution of syringes, diagnostic kits and PPE across UAE/GCC (2025)
  • Safety-engineered syringes (e.g., HMD Dispojekt) and prefilled syringes
  • Home healthcare and outpatient settings (hospitals still 54.8% of demand globally)
  • Sustainable/biodegradable disposables and recycling of biomedical waste

Challenges

  • Wide variance between publisher scopes (global USD 114 bn devices vs USD 505 bn broad disposables) complicates benchmarking
  • Post-pandemic price normalisation: glove ASPs expected to fall at least USD 2 per 1,000 pieces from Q1 FY2027 (CIMB)
  • Buyer concentration (NUPCO, PureHealth, GPOs) pressuring supplier pricing
  • Regulatory registration lead times (SFDA, MOHAP) for new SKUs

What it means for you

  • Hospital leaders: rationalise specifications (one glove, one syringe family, one IV set family), tender by landed cost per unit of use with fill-rate KPIs, and dual-source the top 20 lines — see our five-line scoring.
  • Suppliers: GS1 data, safety-engineered ranges and in-country stock are the differentiators.
  • Investors: regional manufacturing capacity backed by public-procurement preference.
