Executive summary

The UAE healthcare market is the most privatised and insurance-driven in the GCC. Mandatory employer-funded insurance in Abu Dhabi and Dubai, now extending federally, routes most outpatient and a growing share of inpatient volume through private providers, while government systems (PureHealth/SEHA in Abu Dhabi, Dubai Health, Emirates Health Services in the northern emirates) retain tertiary and public-health roles. Published estimates for 2025 range from USD 22.7 bn to USD 32.4 bn; our central estimate is USD 26.0 bn, growing at about 6% to USD 39.1 bn by 2032.

Growth is now structural rather than capacity-led: population growth from new residency programmes, chronic disease, specialist care previously sought abroad (oncology, cardiac, paediatric, fertility), and medical tourism into Dubai and Abu Dhabi. Consolidation has produced a handful of large groups that buy centrally and negotiate hard with insurers — which is why procurement discipline and supply chain visibility have become board-level topics.

Key facts and context

  • Over 98% of UAE residents are covered by mandatory health insurance (Nexdigm)
  • Nearly 60% of licensed UAE healthcare facilities are privately operated; >70% of Dubai's population relies on private care (Nexdigm)
  • UAE operates 173 hospitals and 6,854 health centres (Markntel Advisors)
  • Burjeel Holdings FY2025: revenue AED 5.5 bn, net profit AED 503m, EBITDA AED 1.09 bn, inpatient volumes +11.7% (Gulf News)
  • PureHealth's Daman: 3.4m insured members, H1 2026 GWP AED 5.5 bn (+13%) (Khaleej Times)
  • Abu Dhabi DoH processed >1.6 million insurance claims per month in 2024 (Nexdigm)
  • Dubai expected to face a 1,614 acute-care bed shortfall by 2030 (Nexdigm)
  • Mandatory insurance introduced in Abu Dhabi in 2006, Dubai in 2012, Northern Emirates in 2025 (Gulf News)
  • UAE healthcare technology investment projected to exceed AED 4.8 bn; telehealth market could reach AED 853m (Nexdigm)

GCC and India outlook

Abu Dhabi

PureHealth (SEHA, Daman, NMH, Rafed procurement) is the integrated public-sector platform; Burjeel, NMC and Mediclinic lead private tertiary care; Malaffi health information exchange connects providers. DoH’s Thiqa and basic-plan structures shape what is bought and reimbursed.

Dubai

Dubai Health (formerly DHA facilities) runs public hospitals; private groups (Mediclinic, Aster, American Hospital, King’s College Hospital London, Saudi German, NMC) dominate private beds; Dubai Healthcare City and Dubai Health Experience drive medical tourism; NABIDH is the HIE.

Northern emirates

Emirates Health Services and MOHAP run public facilities; private capacity is growing as mandatory insurance extends; Riayati is the HIE.

India link

Indian groups (Aster, Apollo-affiliated, Medcare/Aster) and Indian clinicians and nurses are embedded in UAE delivery; pharmaceutical and consumable imports from India are significant; UAE groups increasingly source devices and equipment through Indian-linked distributors.

Market dynamics

Growth drivers

  • Mandatory health insurance extended to Northern Emirates from 1 Jan 2025 (basic plans from AED 350), completing nationwide coverage (>98% of residents covered)
  • Chronic disease burden: 67% of residents aged 36-60 pre-diabetic (MoHAP report cited by Nexdigm)
  • Medical tourism: 691,000+ international patients to Dubai; 1.5m+ medical tourists nationally with 2m+ target by 2030
  • Capacity expansion: 10 new hospitals and 150+ clinics added in 2024; Mediclinic AED 120m Airport Road Hospital investment (2025)
  • Genomics/precision medicine: 800,000+ genomes sequenced under Emirati Genome Programme

Restraints

  • Medical inflation: ~12% rise in medical costs projected for 2025 (Nexdigm)
  • High cost of specialised services and shortage of medical professionals outside major cities (Insights10)
  • Hospital concentration in Abu Dhabi and Dubai limits access in Northern Emirates; new facilities there 3-4 years away (Gulf News)
  • Low bed density (2.5 private beds per 1,000 vs OECD 5.5)

Opportunities

  • Northern Emirates specialty hospitals and clinics as insurance-driven demand rises (Arabian Healthcare Group planning multiple RAK clinics)
  • Dubai acute-bed shortfall of 1,614 beds by 2030 invites private capacity
  • Outbound consolidation by UAE groups: PureHealth (Circle Health UK, Hellenic Healthcare), Burjeel Saudi platforms
  • AI and digital health: 40+ predictive AI models in public hospitals; unified digital licensing platform (2026)

Challenges

  • Payer pressure from insurers and employers as premiums and claims volumes (1.6m/month in Abu Dhabi) climb
  • Competition among large private groups driving margin pressure and M&A
  • Workforce dependence on expatriate clinicians and licensing across three regulators (MOHAP, DoH, DHA)

What it means for you

  • Suppliers: MOHAP registration, a DoH/DHA-licensed distributor, insurer-formulary listing and Tatmeen-compliant serialisation (pharma) are the four gates; central tenders at PureHealth (Rafed), Burjeel, Mediclinic and NMC decide most private volume.
  • Operators and investors: growth is in specialist, day-surgery, home care and northern-emirates capacity; licensing timelines are 6–48 months (see our licensing roadmap).
  • Hospital leaders: insurer pressure makes the 25–35% of cost in supplies the lever — see the executive assessment.
