Executive summary

Health insurance is the largest non-life insurance line in the GCC, built on mandatory employer-funded cover in Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Saudi Arabia and now the rest of the UAE. Published estimates for 2025 gross written premiums range from USD 19.4 bn to USD 19.4 bn; our central estimate is USD 22.0 bn, growing at about 9% to USD 40.2 bn by 2032. Saudi Arabia (Bupa Arabia, Tawuniya, MedGulf and others under CCHI) and the UAE (Daman, ADNIC, Orient, Sukoon, Dubai Insurance, Cigna, AXA/GIG) dominate.

Medical inflation — utilisation plus unit-cost growth — runs well above general inflation in both markets, so insurers push back on provider tariffs, require generic substitution and pre-authorisation, and move to value-based contracts. For hospital groups, that pressure lands directly on margin, which is why supply-chain cost — a third of operating cost — has become the main controllable lever.

Key facts and context

  • UAE health claims rose 15.1% in 2025; sector gross loss ratio 65.1%, health loss ratio 75.3% (CBUAE via Khaleej Times)
  • UAE has 58 licensed insurers (22 national traditional, 10 takaful, 26 foreign branches) and 17.3 mn policies; solvency ratio 199% (CBUAE via Khaleej Times)
  • UAE H1 2025: total GWP AED 40.9 bn (+14.5%), health GWP +12.7%; federal health budget AED 5.745 bn (8% of 2025 budget) (Gulf Business)
  • Average UAE health premium rises ~10%/yr; a 10% rise adds AED 250-600 for a single adult, AED 1,200-2,500 for a family of four (Khaleej Times / InsuranceMarket.ae)
  • Saudi insured health lives: 12.1 mn (2023), 13.2 mn (2024), >14 mn (2025) (Sharikat Mubasher; Insurance Authority)
  • Saudi listed insurers 2025: ROE 7.8% (from 14.8%), insurance service margin 2.9%, loss ratio 89.3% (Milliman)
  • Saudi insurance net income 2025 SAR 1.9 bn; average solvency 156.7% (Saudi Insurance Authority via Zawya)
  • Alpen Capital: GCC non-life to reach ~88% of regional premiums by 2030; Saudi largest and fastest-growing (5.9% CAGR) (Khaleej Times)

GCC and India outlook

UAE

Abu Dhabi (since 2006) and Dubai (2014) mandate cover; the federal scheme for the northern emirates (2025) adds the remaining private-sector workforce; Thiqa (nationals) and basic plans set floors; DHA eClaims and Malaffi/NABIDH underpin claims data.

Saudi Arabia

CCHI-regulated mandatory cover for expatriates and growing coverage of nationals through employer schemes; NPHIES standardises claims; Bupa Arabia and Tawuniya hold most of the market; the Health Holding Company reforms will shift more public volume to insurance-like purchasing.

India link

India’s private health insurance and PM-JAY are structurally different (fragmented, package-rate), but GCC insurers’ pre-authorisation and formulary tools are being adopted by Indian insurers; TPAs and claims-tech vendors operate in both.

Market dynamics

Growth drivers

  • Mandatory cover expansion: UAE federal basic scheme (AED 320/yr) for private-sector and domestic workers from 1 Jan 2025 drove health policies +26.1% in 2025 (+59.9% in 2024)
  • Saudi CCHI expansion scenarios lift health premiums from SAR 42 bn (2024) to SAR 65-83 bn by 2030; insured lives 13.2 mn to ~15 mn
  • Medical cost inflation of ~4-8% in UAE plus utilisation growth as insured pool widens; ~40% of policyholders declare a pre-existing condition
  • Expatriate-driven employer group schemes (UAE health segment ~USD 10.1 bn) and Saudi population/Vision 2030 growth (Saudi fastest GCC market, 5.9% CAGR)
  • Digitisation of claims and TPA platforms; Saudi Sehhaty/Nphies-style integration

Restraints

  • Rising loss ratios: Saudi listed insurers' loss ratio 89.3% (2025, up from 86.7%) with net income -40.8%; Bupa Arabia profit -7.5%
  • Price regulation on basic schemes (AED 320 UAE basic plan frozen) limits repricing
  • Thin margins for smaller insurers under claims inflation, reinsurance costs and IFRS 17 expense recognition
  • Saudi solvency margin fell to 156.7% (from 165.7%) amid 440,000+ complaints and SAR 25 mn fines

Opportunities

  • Northern Emirates and remaining GCC states (Bahrain, Oman, Kuwait) still rolling out mandatory cover
  • Saudi regulatory-expansion scenario adds ~SAR 18 bn of health premium by 2030 vs baseline
  • UAE 2026 premium growth estimated at 10-15% after late-2025 price rises (Alpen Capital)
  • Consolidation among 58 UAE insurers and 24 Saudi listed insurers; market highly concentrated in Tawuniya, Bupa Arabia and Al Rajhi Takaful

Challenges

  • Medical inflation outpacing CPI; health claims +15.1% in UAE 2025
  • Pre-existing conditions (diabetes, hypertension) in ~40% of UAE policyholders raising chronic claims
  • Data discrepancies between regulator series (UAE health GWP AED 30 bn vs AED 36.42 bn)
  • Geopolitical risk and reinsurance cost pressure flagged by Alpen Capital for GCC insurers

What it means for you

  • Hospital leaders: insurer tariffs will not rise with your costs; the response is supply-chain cost, clinical pathways and documentation discipline — the executive assessment starts there.
  • Suppliers: insurer formularies and generic-substitution rules decide product access as much as hospital tenders.
  • Investors: claims analytics, TPA and provider-network platforms; insurer–provider integration (PureHealth/Daman model).

Frequently asked questions

How big is the GCC health insurance market?

Published estimates normalised to 2025 range from USD 19.4 bn to USD 19.4 bn. Consulting Wallah’s central estimate is USD 22.0 bn in 2025, rising to about USD 40.2 bn by 2032.

What is the growth rate?

Published CAGRs range from 4.88% to 4.88%; our central case is about 9% a year to 2032.

Which companies lead?

Daman (National Health Insurance Company, PureHealth), ADNIC (Abu Dhabi National Insurance Company), Orient Insurance (Al-Futtaim), Sukoon (formerly Oman Insurance), Dubai Insurance Company, Abu Dhabi National Takaful, Cigna Middle East, MetLife Gulf among others.
