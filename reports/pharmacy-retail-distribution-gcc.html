Executive summary

Pharmacy retail and distribution in the GCC is concentrated and regulated: a few national chains dominate retail in Saudi Arabia (Nahdi, Al Dawaa) and the UAE (Aster, Life, BinSina, Medicina, Boots/Alliance), and a small number of licensed distributors (Pharmalink, Julphar, Gulf Drug, Al Ittihad, Cigalah, Tamer, Banaja, Bin Zagr) hold the agencies through which imported medicines reach the market. Published estimates for 2025 range from USD 8.8 bn to USD 18.9 bn; our central estimate is USD 28.0 bn, growing at about 4% to USD 38.1 bn by 2032.

Growth comes from insurance-covered prescriptions, OTC and wellness categories, chain expansion and e-pharmacy (regulated, delivery-led). Regulation sets maximum prices and margins (MOHAP, SFDA), mandates serialisation (Tatmeen, RSD) and licenses pharmacists; chains are vertically integrating into distribution and private label.

Scope and definitions

Included: retail pharmacy sales (prescription, OTC, wellness, medical consumables sold at retail), e-pharmacy, and the pharmaceutical wholesale/distribution layer. Excluded: hospital pharmacy purchases (in hospital reports), manufacturing. Regulation: MOHAP/DoH/DHA (UAE), SFDA and MoH (Saudi), Emirates Drug Establishment.

Key facts and context

  • Nahdi (~30-35%) and Al Dawaa (~20-25%) together hold roughly half of Saudi pharmacy retail; Nahdi 1,100+ and Al Dawaa ~900 outlets (Argaam Weekend)
  • Nahdi historical EBITDA margin mid-to-high teens; Al Dawaa low-to-mid teens; Western retail pharmacy 4-8% (Argaam Weekend)
  • Nahdi major shareholders: Al Nahdi Holding 38.08%, SEDCO Capital 25% (AGBI)
  • Saudi ~1.7 mn people aged 60+ in 2025 (4.8% of population) (IMARC)
  • Julphar sold Zahrat Al Rawdah pharmacy retail operations to BinDawood Holding; Chefaa raised USD 5.25 mn; PureHealth launched Dawak digital pharmacy (IMARC)
  • Oman has 20 pharma production facilities operating and 18 under construction (JLL via Khaleej Times)
  • Life Pharmacy planned 100 pharmacies and 15 pharmacy hypermarkets in India by end-2024 (Khaleej Times)

GCC and India outlook

Saudi Arabia

Nahdi (listed) and Al Dawaa lead; NUPCO supplies public hospitals, not retail; distributors (Cigalah, Tamer, Banaja, Bin Zagr, Salehiya) hold agencies; e-pharmacy and delivery grew post-COVID under SFDA rules.

UAE

Aster, Life, BinSina and Medicina chains expand with malls and insurer networks; distributors (Pharmalink, Gulf Drug, Al Ittihad, Julphar) control imports; Tatmeen applies at retail; online pharmacy is licensed under DHA/MOHAP.

India link

Indian generics reach GCC retail through these distributors; Indian retail models (Apollo Pharmacy, MedPlus, Tata 1mg) are benchmarks for scale and e-pharmacy; Indian pharmacists are a large share of the GCC workforce.

Market dynamics

Growth drivers

  • Chronic disease burden: diabetes affects ~23.1% of Saudi adults; cardiovascular is 24% of Saudi pharmacy retail
  • Mandatory insurance expansion (UAE nationwide from Jan 2025; Saudi CCHI) and Vision 2030 public health allocations above USD 65 bn
  • Chain consolidation and network expansion: Nahdi opened 59 stores in H1 2025; Aster-Al Hokair JV targets 180+ Saudi pharmacies with USD 250 mn investment
  • Online/omnichannel shift: Nahdi online channel is 25% of revenue; Aster offers sub-60-minute delivery from 40 Dubai pharmacies
  • GCC local manufacturing push (Operation 300B, Saudi fast-track approvals) with biosimilars growing 15-20% p.a.

Restraints

  • Government drug price controls limiting retail margin flexibility (UAE CAGR only 2.4%)
  • Intense competition between chains, independents and online platforms compressing margins
  • High prime real-estate, storage and technology costs in Saudi retail
  • Emirate-specific compliance and data-protection requirements in the UAE

Opportunities

  • Saudi M&A: UPC acquired Kunooz (SAR 100 mn+, 90-110 stores) at ~4-7x EBITDA with two more deals in pipeline; sector trades 8-11x EBITDA
  • Cross-border entry of UAE chains into Saudi (Aster 186 pharmacies in 5 years; Nahdi expanding into UAE)
  • E-pharmacy growing ~14.8% CAGR in GCC vs 2-5% for physical retail; PureHealth Dawak and robotic pharmacies (EHS)
  • Locally produced generics market in GCC to reach USD 14.7 bn by 2032

Challenges

  • Distributor fragmentation: Saudi distribution market only USD 8.5 bn with many mid-size agents; NUPCO centralising public tenders
  • Pharmacist staffing and Saudization requirements for 9,000+ outlets
  • Flagship urban pharmacies earn SAR 8-10 mn/yr while chain EBITDA margins are mid-teens (Nahdi) to low-teens (Al Dawaa), vulnerable to price cuts
  • Published market sizes diverge (Saudi USD 12-14 bn industry estimate vs USD 18.9 bn IMARC)

What it means for you

  • Manufacturers: the distributor decides retail presence; negotiate marketing support, stock commitments and data (sell-out) into the agency agreement.
  • Hospital groups: captive retail pharmacy is a margin line; purchasing should be centralised with hospital procurement to pool volume.
  • Investors: chain consolidation, e-pharmacy and distribution platforms with GDP capability.
