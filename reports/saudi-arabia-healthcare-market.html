Executive summary

Saudi Arabia is the largest healthcare market in the GCC and one of the largest reform programmes in healthcare anywhere. Under Vision 2030’s Health Sector Transformation Program, public hospitals are being corporatised into regional health clusters, the Ministry of Health is becoming a regulator and payer rather than an operator, private and PPP provision is expanding, and NUPCO centralises purchasing for the public sector. Published estimates for 2025 range from USD 76.8 bn to USD 76.8 bn; our central estimate is USD 72.0 bn, growing at about 6% to USD 108 bn by 2032.

Demand is driven by a young but rapidly ageing population, very high chronic-disease prevalence, expanding insurance coverage (CCHI mandatory private insurance for expatriates and a growing share of nationals), and giga-projects with their own health infrastructure. Supply-side, the state is investing in localisation — pharmaceutical and device manufacturing, and a national health workforce — which changes how suppliers must position themselves.

Key facts and context

  • Saudi Arabia accounts for ~60% of GCC healthcare expenditure; government spending >60% of national healthcare expenditure (trade.gov)
  • 499 hospitals and 80,072 beds in 2023; MoH 59%, quasi-government 18%, private 23% of beds (KTrade)
  • HMG 2024 revenue SAR 11.2 bn (18% CAGR 2020-24), 2,888 beds, 74% of revenue from insurance (KTrade)
  • Seha Virtual Hospital remotely supports 130 hospitals; Sehhaty digital platform has 31m users (trade.gov; PharmaKnowl)
  • NUPCO bids require NUPCO prequalification and SFDA product registration (trade.gov)
  • 84,000 new hospital beds needed by 2030 (TASC); Alpen Capital puts Saudi additions at >8,500 beds 2024-2029 (TASC; Alpen Capital)
  • HMG IPO (March 2020) was ~83x oversubscribed with orders near SAR 217 bn; HMG market value ~USD 21.9 bn (Zavis / Argaam)
  • Mouwasat holds ~31% of private beds in the Eastern Province; Dallah's 83% of revenue is from insurance (KTrade)
  • Saudi digital health market USD 2.5 bn (2024) to USD 16.94 bn (2033) per Research and Markets (BusinessWire)

GCC and India outlook

Structure

Twenty-plus health clusters now run public hospitals as corporatised entities under the Health Holding Company; model-of-care reform pushes volume to primary care and virtual care (Seha Virtual Hospital). PPPs cover hospitals, dialysis, radiology, laboratories and logistics. NUPCO handles framework tendering, warehousing and distribution for public entities; private groups buy independently.

Private sector

Dr Sulaiman Al Habib, Mouwasat, Dallah, Fakeeh Care, Al Hammadi, Saudi German, Bupa Arabia and Tawuniya (payers) are listed; expansion is concentrated in Riyadh, Jeddah, the Eastern Province and now secondary cities.

India link

India supplies a large share of Saudi pharmaceutical imports, nursing and clinical workforce, and is a growing source of devices and consumables; Saudi localisation policies favour suppliers willing to manufacture or partner locally.

Market dynamics

Growth drivers

  • Vision 2030 Health Sector Transformation Program: privatise 290 hospitals and 2,300 PHCs; raise private share of delivery (targets of 35% or 65% depending on metric)
  • Health clusters under Health Holding Company (Cabinet Decision 469) with 20+ clusters each serving ~1m people; HHC led by ex-HMG CEO Nasser Al Huqbani
  • Insurance expansion: insured population 36% (2022) to 54% target by 2030; national health insurance for all citizens targeted from 2026
  • Demographics: population 32.2m (2022) to >40m by 2030; 45+ cohort growing 2.4x to ~14m by 2030
  • Government health budget SAR 260 bn in 2025, second only to defence (~20% of spending)

Restraints

  • Hospital market growth modest at 2.5% CAGR (TechSci/R&M) versus faster private-operator growth, reflecting public capacity dominance
  • Insurance enforcement gap: 33% of Saudis and 22% of expats not covered despite mandates
  • Bed density 24.9 per 10,000 vs OECD ~32; Makkah region only 13.4
  • Localisation (Saudization) rules from April 2025 and 40% local-content minimum for government tenders raise supplier compliance burden
  • Import dependence of ~90% for medical devices

Opportunities

  • PPP pipeline of 100+ projects (USD 12.8 bn) across HHC's nine priority areas: primary care, hospitals, medical cities, labs, radiology, pharmacies, rehab, long-term care, home care
  • Listed operators adding 5,400+ beds 2023-2028 (sector private bed capacity +81%); HMG SAR 10 bn programme adding 2,000 beds; Fakeeh SAR 5 bn doubling beds to 1,675
  • Localisation of pharma/device manufacturing (NUPCO SAR 4 bn deal with Novo Nordisk and Sanofi; SIHA/Boston Oncology SAR 3 bn)
  • Digital health: Seha Virtual Hospital supporting 130 hospitals; Sehhaty app 31m users; digital health market USD 2.5 bn (2024) to USD 16.94 bn (2033) per R&M
  • Medical tourism and population growth in Riyadh (8m to 10m by 2030)

Challenges

  • Workforce scaling: 200,000+ new jobs needed alongside Saudization quotas
  • Transition of all public hospitals into clusters under HHC changes procurement counterparties (NUPCO prequalification + SFDA registration required)
  • Private operators' share prices fell 11-41% in the year to May 2025 amid capacity-expansion costs
  • Regional imbalance: Riyadh and Jeddah absorb 71% of new private beds

What it means for you

  • Suppliers: SFDA registration, a licensed local agent, NUPCO prequalification and increasingly a localisation plan are the gates; frameworks are large and multi-year — losing one tender means waiting years.
  • Operators and investors: PPP and cluster outsourcing (dialysis, imaging, labs, long-term care, home care) are the entry points; secondary-city private hospitals are the capacity gap.
  • Hospital leaders: cluster budgets and insurer tariffs are tightening; procurement benchmarking across clusters is the quickest saving.
