Executive summary

The GCC is in the middle of a healthcare construction cycle: new public hospitals and clusters in Saudi Arabia, health components inside giga-projects, private tertiary hospitals in the UAE and Saudi Arabia, and PPP programmes across the region. Published figures depend on what is counted: a 2018 tracker put 707 GCC healthcare projects at USD 60.9 bn; Saudi Arabia alone is projecting USD 65 bn of healthcare investment to 2030 with 290 hospitals and 2,300 primary-care centres slated for private participation; Alpen Capital expects 12,300+ new beds across the GCC by 2029; and 2025 contract awards for healthcare in the GCC ran at roughly USD 3 bn. Our working view is a live pipeline of USD 60–80 bn of healthcare facilities in planning or execution across the six states.

For suppliers and operators, the pipeline is a calendar: equipment, IT and supply-chain contracts for a hospital are tendered 12–24 months before opening, and the operating model (procurement function, formulary, consignment, logistics) is decided earlier still. Projects that set up procurement before opening capture launch-year savings; most do not.

Key facts and context

  • GCC healthcare spending USD 109.1 bn (2024) -> USD 159 bn (2029); Saudi+UAE 82.6% of 2029 spend (Alpen Capital via Arabian Business)
  • GCC hospital beds to reach 140,572 by 2029; 12,317 new beds needed 2024-29, Saudi >8,500 (69%) (Alpen Capital via Arabian Business)
  • Saudi: 290 hospitals and 2,300 primary care facilities slated for privatisation; ~40,000 extra beds needed by 2035; USD 65 bn healthcare infrastructure investment projected (dmg events / Exhibition World)
  • Al Ansar Hospital PPP (Madinah): 244 beds, 20-year DBFOM, Alghanim International + Tamasuk, financed by Alinma, Natixis, Riyad Bank; first Saudi hospital PPP, financial close Mar 2024 (Gulf Construction)
  • Saudi National Privatisation Strategy (Jan 2026) targets USD 64 bn private capital by 2030 across 147 opportunities (MEED)
  • Saudi healthcare market ~SAR 250 bn (USD 67 bn)/yr (PwC); digital health >USD 1 bn in 2024 heading to USD 5 bn by 2033 (AGBI)
  • Global healthcare construction pipeline USD 724.5 bn (Sep 2025), 56.9% in execution (GlobalData via GlobeNewswire)
  • Historical GCC pipeline baselines: USD 60.9 bn / 707 projects (BNC, 2018); USD 55.2 bn MENA with 37 mega hospitals (USD 28.2 bn, 22,500 beds) under way (Alpen, 2017); USD 50.5 bn Middle East (MEED, 2013) (Arabian Business; MEConstructionNews; MEED)
  • Aster DM secured AED 265 mn EDB financing to build two new Dubai hospitals; plans USD 250 mn Saudi expansion over 3-5 years (Gulf News; AGBI)
  • Knight Frank: 47% of Saudi respondents prefer private hospitals for elective procedures; 43% of households use home care; 83% insured (Knight Frank)

GCC and India outlook

Saudi Arabia

Health clusters are building and replacing public hospitals; giga-projects (NEOM, Red Sea, Qiddiya, Diriyah, ROSHN) include hospitals and clinics; PPP programmes cover hospitals, dialysis, imaging and labs; King Salman Medical City and new medical cities anchor regional capacity.

UAE

Abu Dhabi (PureHealth/SEHA expansions, Burjeel, M42-linked facilities), Dubai (Dubai Health’s new hospitals, private tertiary projects, Dubai Healthcare City Phase 2), northern emirates public hospitals; developer-led health districts.

Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain

Qatar’s post-World Cup health projects; Kuwait’s large public hospital programme (Jaber, Al Sabah expansions, regional hospitals); Oman’s Sultan Qaboos Medical City and private investment under Vision 2040; Bahrain’s private hospital projects.

India link

Indian hospital groups, contractors, medical-equipment planners and workforce suppliers are embedded in GCC projects; Indian manufacturers can target the fit-out and consumables tenders that follow construction.

Market dynamics

Growth drivers

  • GCC healthcare expenditure rising from USD 109.1 bn (2024) to USD 159 bn (2029), 7.8% CAGR, with CHE/GDP 5% -> 5.7%
  • Bed shortfalls: Saudi 2.0 beds/1,000 vs 2.9 global; Riyadh gap ~15,300 beds by 2040; GCC needs 12,317 new beds by 2029
  • Ageing/demographics: GCC population over 50 from 12.7% to 13.8% (2024-29); Saudi 65+ nationals 984k (2024) to 2.15 mn (2040); Saudi population 35 mn+ heading to 37.4 mn
  • Privatisation and PPP policy: Saudi target to lift private share of healthcare from 40% to 65% by 2030; 290 hospitals and 2,300 PHCs slated for privatisation; National Privatisation Strategy targets USD 64 bn private capital by 2030 across 147 opportunities
  • Mandatory/free health insurance: 83% of Saudi residents insured; all Saudi nationals to get free health insurance from 2026

Restraints

  • Reliance on expatriate clinical workforce and shortage of tertiary/specialised care (Alpen Capital)
  • Long-term-care patients occupy 15-20% of acute beds with LTC occupancy >90% in Saudi, blocking acute capacity
  • High build cost benchmarks (~AED 46 mn per added active bed cited from a US comparator) and medical inflation
  • Dependence on imported medical supplies and equipment
  • Outbound medical tourism: 31% of Saudi nationals and 24% of expats have travelled abroad for treatment

Opportunities

  • Saudi PPP pipeline: Al Ansar 244-bed hospital (20-yr DBFOM, financial close Mar 2024), SABIC Behavioural Health Hospital USD 1 bn/150 beds (15-yr, Jun 2026), CKD & National Dialysis Services PPP (EOI May 2026), 224 PPP healthcare centres planned
  • Long-term care and senior living: 12,000 LTC beds needed by 2030 in Saudi; LTC named a national investment priority
  • Private operator expansion: Almoosa and SMC (1,300 beds) to double capacity in four years; Aster USD 250 mn Saudi expansion; Burjeel day-surgery centres in Riyadh
  • UAE community-integrated hospitals (Arada Aljada, Jouri Hills) and Abu Dhabi primary-care build-out (PureHealth 22 SEHA clinics in 2026)
  • Branded international operators partnering with local groups (Johns Hopkins Aramco oncology centre Dhahran, Feb 2025)

Challenges

  • Pipeline value concentrated in Saudi (51%) and dependent on government budget cycles and giga-project sequencing
  • Only ~USD 3 bn of GCC healthcare contracts out to bid in Q1 2025 despite large announced pipelines
  • Project cost and schedule overruns on medical cities (historical examples: King Faisal Medical City SR4 bn / 1,350 beds; King Khalid Medical City USD 1.2 bn)
  • Competition and expected M&A as larger operators acquire smaller providers (Alpen Capital)

What it means for you

  • Suppliers: track projects by expected opening date and position 18 months ahead for equipment, 12 months for IT and supply chain, 6 months for consumables and pharmacy stocking.
  • Operators and investors: PPP and operator contracts are the entry route into public capacity; design procurement and supply chain into the operating model before commissioning — see setup, launch and regulatory.
  • Hospital leaders: new sites are the opportunity to standardise item masters, formularies and contracts group-wide from day one.
