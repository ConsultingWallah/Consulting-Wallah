Executive summary

The medical imaging equipment market — MRI, CT, ultrasound, X-ray and fluoroscopy, mammography, nuclear medicine and PET, plus the imaging IT and service contracts attached — grows with bed capacity, cancer and cardiac programmes, and replacement cycles. Published estimates for the 2025 global market range from USD 46.8 bn to USD 46.8 bn; our central estimate is USD 46.8 bn, growing at about 5% to USD 65.0 bn by 2032.

The GCC buys premium configurations through central tenders and large groups; India is the fastest-growing large market with a widening mid-tier (Mindray, United Imaging, Wipro GE, Siemens Healthineers India, Trivitron, Allengers) and strong refurbished and PPP segments. In both, the total cost of ownership — service, consumables, software licences, upgrades — exceeds the purchase price over seven years, which is where procurement wins or loses.

Key facts and context

  • Global medical imaging market USD 46.8 bn in 2025, to USD 71.2 bn by 2034 (4.78% CAGR); X-ray systems largest product segment (IMARC)
  • GCC medical imaging systems market USD 2.2 bn (2024), 9.5% CAGR to USD 3.5 bn by 2031 (Ken Research)
  • GCC faces projected shortage of 18,000 radiologists/imaging technicians; high-end MRI >USD 3 mn (Ken Research)
  • Saudi imaging equipment market USD 439 mn (2025); GE, Siemens, Philips ~55-60% share; hospitals 57.3% of demand (IMARC)
  • India: ~70% of medical devices imported (>USD 7 bn in 2023); domestic production covers <15% of X-ray/CT/MRI demand; India ~1.5% of global medtech vs China 14%, US 40% (GrowthJockey (Sep 2025))
  • India medical imaging market USD 1.52 bn (2025); X-ray 28.7%, hospitals 65.4%, South India 29.1% (IMARC)
  • India PLI for medical devices: Rs 3,420 cr outlay, 27 projects, Rs 1,153 cr invested, 14 MSMEs (Indian Pharma Post)
  • United Imaging installed base: >32,000 products in 14,000+ hospitals across 75+ countries (Feb 2025) (Macau Business / PR)
  • GCC: >USD 32 bn allocated to new hospitals/clinics; USD 1.2 bn projected investment in MRI and CT; healthcare spend to exceed USD 104 bn (Ken Research)

GCC and India outlook

GCC

NUPCO, Rafed and large private groups tender multi-site fleets; Saudi clusters outsource radiology under PPPs; AI-enabled scanners and photon-counting CT are being adopted early; service contracts are the recurring spend to benchmark.

India

Demand from hospital chains, diagnostic chains (Dr Lal, Metropolis, Vijaya, Aarthi) and public PPPs (state radiology PPPs); PLI-backed domestic manufacturing of CT/MRI components; refurbished premium systems are a large segment; AERB licensing applies.

Market dynamics

Growth drivers

  • Vision 2030 healthcare investment in Saudi (SAR 500 bn cited) and NEOM smart-hospital initiative; GCC healthcare spend expected to exceed USD 104 bn
  • Chronic disease burden: cardiovascular disease ~37% of GCC deaths; rising oncology and cardiology early-detection demand
  • AI-integrated imaging and autonomous X-ray/ultrasound (GE HealthCare-NVIDIA partnership, Philips CT 5300, Mindray MiCo+ with SEHA Virtual Hospital)
  • Hospital pipeline: >USD 32 bn allocated to new hospitals and clinics in GCC (Ken Research)
  • India PLI scheme (Rs 3,420 cr outlay) enabling local MRI, CT, LINAC, mammography and C-arm production; ageing population (60+ to reach 1.4 bn globally by 2030)

Restraints

  • High capital cost: high-end MRI systems exceed USD 3 mn; high ownership costs
  • Workforce gap: projected shortage of 18,000 radiologists and imaging technicians in GCC
  • Import dependence: India domestic manufacturing covers <15% of demand for X-ray, CT and MRI; ~70% of all devices imported; imported devices cost 30-80% more
  • Radiation exposure, contrast allergy concerns and product recalls (DelveInsight)
  • Regulatory hurdles and data-privacy constraints on cloud/AI imaging in GCC

Opportunities

  • Portable and hand-held imaging growing fastest (Saudi hand-held 8.9% CAGR, portable 7.6%); telemedicine growing 24% annually in GCC
  • Chinese vendors (United Imaging, Mindray, Neusoft) expanding in GCC via Arab Health 2025 partnerships (Al Mana Group Saudi, Jaber Al-Ahmad Center Kuwait)
  • India 'Make in India' imaging: first indigenous 1.5T MRI (AIIMS trials, Mar 2025); Wipro GE SIGNA Prime Elite MRI made in PLI facility (Mar 2026)
  • Diagnostic imaging centre chains in Saudi (22.6% share, 6.8% CAGR) and private hospital entrants (AKDITAL Riyadh)
  • Value partnerships / managed equipment services (Siemens-Prisma Health model) for public health clusters

Challenges

  • Big-3 concentration: GE, Siemens, Philips hold ~55-60% of Saudi imaging revenue, limiting negotiating leverage
  • India imaging market growing only 4.4% CAGR despite 15-20% device-market growth, reflecting price erosion and import substitution
  • Service and spare-parts dependence on OEMs for installed base
  • GCC procurement via central tenders (NUPCO) requiring localisation commitments

What it means for you

  • Hospital leaders: tender on 7-year TCO (equipment + service + uptime guarantees + consumables + software), with multi-vendor service options and buy-back terms; standardise modalities across sites to pool service.
  • Suppliers: uptime guarantees, local engineers and financing (equipment-as-a-service) win over list-price discounts.
  • Investors: radiology PPPs and diagnostic chains in Saudi secondary cities and Indian tier-2 cities; refurbishment and service businesses.
