Executive summary

Home healthcare — skilled nursing, rehabilitation, home dialysis and infusion, respiratory support, remote monitoring and post-acute care delivered in the patient’s home — is one of the fastest-growing service segments in the GCC and India. Published estimates for 2025 range from USD 8.8 bn to USD 8.8 bn; our central estimate is USD 8.8 bn, growing at about 9% to USD 16.1 bn by 2032.

Three forces drive it: payers want long-stay and post-acute patients out of expensive hospital beds (UAE insurers and DoH/DHA have home-care tariffs; Saudi clusters contract home care); ageing and chronic disease increase the need; and hospital groups see home care as both a capacity release and a new service line. In India, organised home-care providers have emerged in metros with hospital partnerships and insurer pilots.

Scope and definitions

Included: licensed home nursing and allied health, home medical equipment and supplies, home dialysis and infusion services, remote monitoring tied to clinical services. Excluded: unlicensed domestic care; consumer devices without services. Licensing: DoH and DHA home-care licences (UAE); MoH (Saudi); state clinical establishment acts (India).

Key facts and context

  • Dubai had 5,931 licensed health facilities and 70,566 licensed professionals in H1 2026; home care facilities 249 (+22.7%) (DHA via Khaleej Times)
  • Top four Saudi organised home-care providers (Saudi German Health, ADEED, Dallah, IMC) hold ~35-40% of the services market (IMARC)
  • Saudi government goal: shift 30% of healthcare delivery from hospitals to community settings (IMARC)
  • Saudi MoH: 51 mn+ Sehhaty virtual consultations, 14 mn+ Wasfaty users and 40 mn+ e-prescriptions in 2024 (Arab News)
  • UAE home healthcare: clinical management systems hold 51.9% of home-care software segment (2025) (Mordor Intelligence)
  • India home healthcare market grew from USD 9.74 bn (2024) to USD 11.90 bn (2025); diagnostics expected to generate highest revenue (Mordor Intelligence)
  • WHO projects 80+ population to reach 426 mn by 2050, a core driver cited for home care (Coherent Market Insights)

GCC and India outlook

GCC

UAE providers (Burjeel, Mediclinic, NMC, Aster, Amana, HealthPlus, Emirates Home Nursing) are licensed by DoH/DHA; insurers cover defined home-care packages; Saudi clusters outsource home care under the model-of-care reform; long-stay discharge programmes are the volume driver.

India

Portea, Nightingales (Medwell), HealthCare atHome, Apollo HomeCare and hospital-linked providers serve metros; insurer coverage is nascent; demand is strong for post-surgical, elderly and critical-care-at-home services.

Market dynamics

Growth drivers

  • UAE nationwide mandatory health insurance in force from 1 Jan 2025 (Mordor estimates +2.8 pts to CAGR) and Saudi CCHI confirming cover for home services such as post-op IV medication and wound care
  • Saudi Vision 2030 Health Sector Transformation Program goal of shifting 30% of care delivery from hospitals to community/home settings
  • Chronic disease burden: Saudi has 5.3 mn diabetes cases (2024) and >5.1 mn hypertensive adults; chronic respiratory conditions are 41% of UAE home-care demand
  • Remote patient monitoring, telehealth and AI triage (Saudi Sehhaty platform logged 51 mn+ virtual consultations in 2024)
  • Private equity / PPP inflows and hospital-group diversification into post-acute home care

Restraints

  • Shortage of licensed home-care clinicians; largely expatriate workforce with Saudization pressure (Mordor: -1.6 pts CAGR in UAE)
  • Limited insurer reimbursement for devices and non-critical home services (-1.2 pts)
  • Fragmented emirate-level regulation (DHA / DoH / MOHAP) complicating inter-emirate operations (-0.9 pts)
  • Hospital-centric cultural preferences among patients and families

Opportunities

  • Saudi organised home-care services (~USD 1.8 bn, 42.7% of market) growing fastest at ~8.2% CAGR; top four providers hold only 35-40%
  • Saudi National Health Insurance Scheme expansion to nationals expected to add covered lives by 2027
  • UAE October 2024 unified licensing reform easing credential transfers between emirates
  • Cancer & palliative care fastest-growing UAE condition segment (11.6% CAGR); India market growing 18% CAGR with low concentration

Challenges

  • High operating costs and logistics complexity of dispersed home visits
  • Mandatory electronic records / Malaffi interoperability compliance in Abu Dhabi and ISO 13485 device registration
  • Lack of awareness and scarcity of skilled professionals in India
  • Uneven definitions: GCC estimates range from USD 1.2 bn (UAE services-focused) to USD 8.8 bn (GCC products+services)

What it means for you

  • Hospital leaders: a home-care arm releases beds and keeps the patient in the system; the economics depend on nurse utilisation, travel time and a disciplined supply chain for consumables and equipment at home.
  • Investors: insurer-contracted post-acute and chronic programmes; home dialysis and infusion with pharma partnerships.
  • Suppliers: home-use devices, portable oxygen, infusion pumps and consumable kits are a distinct channel with its own registration and service requirements.
